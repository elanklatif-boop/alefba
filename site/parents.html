For parents and adult learners

How Alefba works, what it keeps, what the plans cost, and how its AI talks are kept safe for children.

In short: the alphabet and the first 3 levels are free and need no account. For the AI talks, a parent signs in with their email, and every family gets 3 free talks. In a class whose teacher has the school plan with talks, the teacher agrees for the class instead, and nobody signs in. A plan opens all 16 levels, the talks and speaking practice. Your child's name and progress stay on your own device unless they join a teacher's class or you turn on Progress on every device, and the chats aren't saved.

What is Alefba?

Alefba helps anyone learn to read and speak Persian: the letters, everyday words, how people really say them at home, and short practice conversations with friendly characters. It's built so children aged 7 to 14 can use it on their own, there's a little mode with pictures and tracing for ages 4 to 6, and adults use it too: when you start, you choose whether a child or an adult is learning. Here's the version of this page for adult learners.

Learning as an adult?

Everything on this page applies to you as well, with you as the account holder: you sign in with your own email for the talks, the free talks and plans are the same, and your name and progress stay on your device. The conversations are different (a coworker, your in-laws, a taxi driver instead of a classmate and a grandmother), and the characters are told they're talking with an adult, but they keep the same rules: nothing unsuitable for children, and no asking for personal details.

What does it keep?

On the device your child uses, and only there: the first name or nickname they type, their lessons and scores, and their settings. These are stored in the browser and not sent to us, unless your child joins a class or you turn on progress on every device (both below). You can clear them with Reset all progress in the Review tab.

If your child joins a teacher's class (with a code from the teacher, after you answer a quick question to say yes), the class keeps the first name or nickname typed when joining, and a summary of progress: which lessons are done, which days had practice, and the streak. Only that class's teacher can see it, and nothing from the talks is ever shared. If the class has the school plan with talks, your child can also have 5 talks a month with the characters, without you signing in: the teacher agrees to this for the class when buying it, and tells families first. If you'd rather your child didn't, tell the teacher, or have your child leave the class. Leaving the class, in the Review tab, deletes it straight away. It's also deleted if the teacher deletes the class or their account, or once the app hasn't been opened for over a year.

If you turn on Progress on every device (in the Review tab, once you're signed in, after a quick question to say yes), Alefba keeps each learner's first name or nickname, their lessons and scores, their practice days and their settings with your sign-in, so they show on every device where you sign in. Nothing from the talks is kept. Turning it off, in the Review tab, deletes these copies straight away (the progress on each device stays). They're also deleted when you delete your account, or if no device has synced for over a year.

If you sign in for the talks, Alefba's server keeps your email address, which plan your family has, and the time each talk started, so it can count talks (those times are deleted after about 100 days). It never keeps what is said in a talk. Sign-in codes are sent through Resend, an email delivery service. Before a code is sent, a quick robot check from Cloudflare (called Turnstile) makes sure a person is asking, so nobody can use the site to send lots of emails.

Payments are handled by Stripe, so Alefba never sees your card number. One cookie keeps you signed in on that device. Alefba has no advertising and no tracking cookies. To count how many people visit, the website uses Cloudflare Web Analytics, which uses no cookies and collects nothing that identifies you or your child. The app also counts, as daily totals only, how many learners open it, how many come back, and how many lessons are started and finished. Those totals say nothing about who anyone is.

If you ask for a reminder email after a lesson, Alefba keeps your email address so it can send one short email the day after a lesson, when the next one is ready. No more come unless there was a lesson the day before. Nothing about your child is sent or kept with it. The emails are sent through Resend. Each one has a link to stop them, and so does the Review tab; stopping them deletes your address, and so does deleting your account. Otherwise it's deleted 60 days after the last email.

To delete your account, open the Plans tab, then your account (See the plans or Manage my plan), then Delete account, and enter the code we email you. That ends any plan, removes your email address and your family's talk counts from Alefba, and deletes your customer record at Stripe (Stripe keeps its own records of past payments). The only thing Alefba keeps is a scrambled code made from your email address, used just to remember that the free talks were used, so they aren't given again. Any progress kept for Progress on every device is deleted too. If you made classes in teacher mode, they're deleted, along with the progress learners shared in them (a school plan for a deleted class ends with it).

How do the AI talks work?

In a talk, your child chats with a character, like a new classmate or a grandmother, using the Persian words they've learned. The character's replies are written by Claude, an AI made by Anthropic. Every talk is labeled AI on screen, and the characters never pretend to be real people.

  • Talks start only after a parent signs in with their email on that device, using a code we send, or in a class whose teacher has the school plan with talks (and has agreed to them for the class). Signing out, or leaving the class, turns talks off again.
  • What your child types in a talk is sent through the Alefba server to Claude so it can write the reply. The Alefba server doesn't save the chats.
  • Your child's name is never sent with a talk, and neither is your email address. Email addresses, phone numbers and links your child types are removed before anything is sent.
  • Anthropic handles the messages under its commercial terms, which don't allow it to train its AI on them. It deletes them within 30 days, except in limited cases, such as a message flagged for a safety review. See how long Anthropic keeps this data.
  • With a plan, children can also practice speaking. In lessons, they say a word out loud and hear their own voice next to the app's recording; that recording stays on your device and is never sent anywhere. In talks, they can say a reply instead of typing it: that recording goes to Cloudflare's speech-to-text, which turns it into text and then deletes it, and Cloudflare doesn't keep it or use it to train its AI. The app asks your browser for the microphone first, and you can turn it off in the browser's settings at any time.
  • With a plan, talks can also be spoken: the character's lines are read aloud by Microsoft's Persian voices (Azure Speech). Only the character's own words are sent to Microsoft, as text, never your child's voice or messages, and Microsoft doesn't keep them. Voice mode is a switch inside each talk, and you can leave it off.
  • To stop the service being overused, the server briefly keeps the internet address a request came from, in memory only, for up to an hour. To limit sign-in emails, it also counts them under a scrambled code made from that address, deleted within three days.

What do the plans cost?

The alphabet and the first 3 levels are free, and every family gets 3 free talks. A plan opens all 16 levels (252 words, 48 conversations and 16 short stories) and the AI talks:

Every plan also includes speaking practice: your child says words out loud and compares their voice with the app's recording.

Every plan also includes Voice mode in talks: the character speaks, your child listens and answers out loud, with Slower, Show words and Hint buttons, so a talk can be a real spoken conversation.

Alefba can be added to a phone's home screen like an app (lessons and sounds then work without internet), and several learners can share one device, each with their own progress. One sign-in and plan covers the whole family.

Plans renew by themselves (monthly or yearly) until you cancel. To cancel, switch plans or update your card, open the Plans tab, then Manage my plan. When you cancel, your plan stays until the end of the time you've paid for. Plans and payments are for grown-ups, so the app asks a quick sum before showing them, and switching plans or deleting the account also needs a code we email to you.

The rules, in short

  • A parent or guardian signs in and looks after the account. Children use Alefba with their permission.
  • Plans renew by themselves until you cancel. If prices ever change, your plan keeps the price you signed up for.
  • Refunds: if something isn't right, write to us within 14 days of a payment and we'll refund it.
  • Unlimited means up to 30 talks a day, to keep things fair for everyone. We may pause an account that misuses the talks, for example by trying to make the characters say harmful things.

How are the talks kept safe?

Every character is told to keep everything suitable for young children, to steer kindly away from anything upsetting, and never to ask for personal details such as an address, school or phone number. Children are also reminded on screen not to share those things.

AI can still make mistakes. Like anything online, it's a good idea to look at a talk together now and then and to remind your child never to share personal information.

If a talk ever shows something that worries you, please tell us (see below) so we can fix it. Chats aren't saved, so a screenshot or a short description helps.

Questions?

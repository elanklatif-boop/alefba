How Persian verbs get shorter when people talk

Mikhâham becomes mikhâm, miravam becomes miram, and khub ast becomes khube. The short forms you'll hear in every Iranian home, and the book forms they come from.

When people talk, many Persian verbs get shorter. Mikhâham (I want) is said mikhâm, miravam (I go) is said miram, and miguyam (I say) is said migam. The written forms are still how they're spelled. The short forms are how everyone talks.

Why does spoken Persian sound so different from the book?

Much of the difference is in the verbs. Long written forms lose a syllable, or their ending changes, so a sentence like نمی‌دانم کجا می‌روم nemidânam kojâ miravam (I don't know where I'm going) comes out as نمی‌دونم کجا می‌رم nemidunam kojâ miram. If you only learn the long forms, you'll understand the news but family conversations will feel fast.

“I” verbs: the most common short forms

The big word is how people say it. Tap ▶ to hear it, and the small ▶ to hear the book form where there is one.

  • می‌خوامmikhâm: I wantIn books: می‌خواهم (mikhâham)
  • می‌رمmiram: I goIn books: می‌روم (miravam)
  • می‌گمmigam: I sayIn books: می‌گویم (miguyam)
  • می‌دمmidam: I giveIn books: می‌دهم (midaham)
  • می‌تونمmitunam: I canIn books: می‌توانم (mitavânam)
  • نمی‌دونمnemidunam: I don't knowIn books: نمی‌دانم (nemidânam)

Some verbs don't change much, like می‌کنم mikonam (I do) and می‌خورم mikhoram (I eat), so you can use them as they are.

“He” and “she” verbs: -ad becomes -e

Persian uses the same verb for he and she. In writing it ends in -ad, and when people talk it ends in -e:

  • می‌کنهmikone: he or she doesIn books: می‌کند (mikonad)
  • می‌خورهmikhore: he or she eatsIn books: می‌خورد (mikhorad)
  • می‌رهmire: he or she goesIn books: می‌رود (miravad)
  • میادmiyâd: he or she comesIn books: می‌آید (miâyad)
  • می‌شهmishe: it becomes, it's possibleIn books: می‌شود (mishavad)

What happens to ast (is) in spoken Persian?

After a word, ast usually shrinks to -e: khub ast (it's good) is said khube, and sard ast (it's cold) is said sarde.

  • خوبهkhube: it's goodIn books: خوب است (khub ast)
  • سردهsarde: it's coldIn books: سرد است (sard ast)
  • بزرگهbozorge: it's bigIn books: بزرگ است (bozorg ast)

What is ro in spoken Persian?

The little word را râ marks the object of a sentence (the thing something is done to). When people talk, it becomes -o after a consonant and -ro after a vowel:

  • کتابو بدهketâbo bede: give (me) the bookIn books: کتاب را بده (ketâb râ bedeh)
  • اینو ببینino bebin: look at thisIn books: این را ببین (in râ bebin)
  • چایی رو بیارchâyi ro biyâr: bring the teaIn books: چای را بیاور (chây râ biyâvar)

Should I learn the long or the short verbs?

Both. Use the short forms when you talk, and learn to recognise the long ones, because that's how they're written in books, signs, the news and subtitles. The vowels change in the same way as in nân and nun, which is why mitavânam becomes mitunam: see why nân becomes nun.

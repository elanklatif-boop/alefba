How to say thank you in Persian

Every everyday way, from mersi to dastet dard nakone: what each one means, when Iranians use it, and how to reply.

The everyday way to say thank you in Persian is mersi (مرسی). Mamnun (ممنون) is a little more polite, and kheyli mersi or kheyli mamnun means thank you very much. To thank someone for their effort, say dastet dard nakone (دستت درد نکنه).

What are the ways to say thank you in Persian?

Mersi and mamnun are the everyday ones. Dastet dard nakone thanks someone for their effort, ghorbunet beram is loving, sharmande means you've done too much, and moteshakeram is formal.

  • مرسیmersi: thanksThe most common everyday thanks, from French “merci.” For friends, family and shops.
  • ممنونmamnun: thanks (a little more polite)Literally “grateful.” Good with anyone.
  • دستت درد نکنهdastet dard nakone: thank you for your troubleLiterally “may your hand not hurt.” After someone cooks, helps or gives you something.
  • قربونت برمghorbunet beram: thank you so much, dearLiterally “may I be sacrificed for you.” Loving: for family and close friends.
  • شرمندهsharmande: you're too kind / sorryLiterally “I'm embarrassed”: you've done too much for me.
  • متشکرمmoteshakeram: thank you (formal)For speeches, letters and polite situations with strangers.

Even more formal than moteshakeram is sepâsgozâram (سپاسگزارم), which you'll mostly see in writing.

How do you say thank you very much in Persian?

Add kheyli (very): kheyli mersi or kheyli mamnun (خیلی ممنون). Both mean thank you very much.

What does dastet dard nakone mean?

It literally means “may your hand not hurt.” It thanks someone for the effort they put in, such as cooking a meal, carrying something or giving a gift, and it's one of the warmest everyday thanks.

To be more polite, or to thank more than one person, say dastetun dard nakone (دستتون درد نکنه). The usual reply is saret dard nakone, “may your head not hurt.”

How do you say you're welcome in Persian?

Say khâhesh mikonam. If you gave them something, you can say ghâbel nadâre (it's nothing), and if they're thanking you for food, nush-e jân (enjoy!).

  • خواهش می‌کنمkhâhesh mikonam: you're welcomeAlso means “please.”
  • قابل ندارهghâbel nadâre: it's nothingWhen someone thanks you for something you gave them.
  • نوش جانnush-e jân: enjoy!When someone thanks you for food.
  • سرت درد نکنهsaret dard nakone: the reply to dastet dard nakoneLiterally “may your head not hurt.”

Is mersi a Persian word?

It came from French, but it's been part of everyday Persian for a long time, and it's the word most Iranians use to say thanks. In formal speech some people prefer moteshakeram or sepâsgozâram.

Which thank you should I use?

With friends and family, mersi. When someone did something for you, dastet dard nakone. With someone older, mamnun or dastetun dard nakone. In a speech or a letter, moteshakeram.

Thanking people is also part of taarof, the Persian way of being polite, where offers, refusals and thanks go back and forth.
